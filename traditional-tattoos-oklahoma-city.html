About the style

Black outlines and a limited palette.

American traditional tattooing is known for prominent black outlines and a limited color palette. The New York Historical’s overview of the style describes those features and the circulation of flash: sheets of prepared tattoo designs displayed in shops. Eagles are among the subjects discussed in that history. In the opening photograph, the red sun is a large block of color behind the eagle, while black lines divide the feather rows and yellow marks the beak and talons.

Compare that eagle with the upper-arm example farther down this page. Both have spread wings, a pale head, and curved talons, but their wing angles and proportions differ. When choosing references, point out the features you like in each: a broad wingspan, a steep wing angle, large areas of black, or a small number of colored accents. Those observations give you specific choices to discuss for your own drawing.

Core design choices

Four choices to discuss for your design.

Use the photographs to compare outlines, overall shapes, color areas, and orientation. Bring the features you prefer into the consultation.

01

Outline

Look at the lines around the eagle’s wing tips and the animal’s open mouth. They mark separate feathers, teeth, and facial features within larger areas of black. If you bring a detailed reference, ask which edges need to be outlined at your proposed tattoo size.

02

Silhouette

Start with the outside shape. The eagle with the red sun has a broad wingspan, while the animal head has pointed ears and a jagged border around the face. Decide which part of your subject matters most: the full body, a head, or another recognizable feature. Put that choice in your brief.

03

Contrast

The eagle with the red sun pairs a red background with black feathers and a pale head. In the animal head, orange eyes and teal details sit within a mostly black shape. Share whether you prefer large color areas or small accents, and identify any colors you want to leave out.

04

Placement

The gallery eagle extends along the upper arm, with the wing tips above the head and talons below it. Bring a measurement for the area you want tattooed and mention any neighboring work. Ask about orientation, spacing, and whether the drawing needs a different size for that location.

Custom traditional work

Prepare a clear brief for your consultation.

Start with a sentence that names the subject and its main feature. For example, “an eagle with raised wings and red accents on my upper arm” gives you several decisions to discuss. Add details that carry personal meaning, such as a particular flower or a name. If you have several ideas, mark which is essential and which is optional so the conversation has a clear starting point.

  • Name the main subject and choose a full figure, head, or single object. Include a preferred pose or expression if it matters to you.
  • List any lettering, dates, or secondary symbols. Bring the exact wording and spelling, and explain which additions are essential.
  • Choose a color direction: strong colored areas, a few accents, or a black and gray proposal. Include colors you especially want or want omitted.
  • Give the intended body area and approximate width and height in inches. Mention nearby tattoos and any plans to add more work around it.
  • Bring a few references with a short note for each: outline, pose, palette, or spacing. Identify the feature you want to discuss from each example.

Traditional tattoo FAQ

Useful answers before your consultation.

Can a personal idea be drawn in a traditional style?+

Tornado Tattoo lists custom tattoo design among its services. Bring the personal subject and explain what makes it recognizable to you. Ask which features can be translated into bold outlines and a compact palette at your chosen size. If a name, date, or small object is essential, mention it at the start so it can be discussed with the main image.

Do traditional tattoos have to use bright color?+

Color is a choice to discuss early, including a black and gray proposal if that is your preference. The photographs here show red, yellow-orange, and teal used alongside large black areas. For a version without colored accents, ask how the subject would be drawn. Compare the outlines, dark areas, and spaces between details as well as the palette when choosing references.

Can Josh create a matching set or connected collection?+

Bring the larger plan to the consultation and ask whether the proposed set is suitable for the subjects and placements. Show any existing pieces you want the new work to sit beside. Note which features you want repeated, such as line weight, a shared palette, or similar scale. If you expect to add tattoos later, include the areas you want to reserve and ask about spacing before selecting the first design.

Are walk-ins available for traditional tattoos?+

Tornado Tattoo’s service list includes walk-ins when available. Call (405) 601-4445 before coming in and describe the subject, approximate size, and body area. Ask whether there is time for your project that day or whether a consultation and later appointment would be more appropriate.
