What determines the plan

Plan the new design around the existing ink.

A cover-up places a new design over existing work. Tattooing 101’s design guide describes aligning the new drawing with the original tattoo and planning around its dark and light areas. Ask to review this relationship at the intended size. Identify the old lines or filled areas you want disguised, and compare their position with the proposed outlines, shadows, and highlights. Include the surrounding skin in the discussion.

Be specific about the change you want. You might want to replace the subject, revise selected details, add color, or connect the piece to nearby work. The hero photograph shows the same portrait outline in two views, with broad red and black areas in the right-hand image. Use a reference like this to explain whether you want to retain recognizable parts of a design or explore a different subject. Bring several replacement ideas and list the features that matter most, including any limits on size, darkness, colors, or placement.

Cover-up factors

What to review at your cover-up consultation

Prepare photographs of the whole area and ask Josh to assess it in person. Review the proposed design alongside the existing tattoo, with attention to these four factors.

01

Existing ink and previous work

Show the full tattoo, including its darkest filled areas, faded lines, and remaining colors. Mention when it was done, any later reworking, and previous laser treatments. Explain exactly what you want changed. Ask how the proposed design relates to the parts you want disguised and whether any existing features would remain part of the new composition.

02

Size and available space

Measure the existing tattoo’s height and width, and identify the furthest boundaries you would accept for the new work. Ask to review the full outline at the proposed size and from different angles. Include nearby tattoos in the discussion. If you are considering a future sleeve or larger piece, explain where you want the cover-up to fit within that plan.

03

Subject and design details

Choose subjects you want to wear, and bring references showing their shapes, colors, and distinguishing details. Discuss where a face, flower center, wing pattern, or ornamental border would sit in relation to the old tattoo. Ask which features the proposed size can accommodate and which parts of the idea need adjustment. Identify any subjects or decorative elements you want excluded.

04

Skin condition and scarring

Point out raised, irritated, or sensitive areas and mention any history of keloid scars. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that tattooing can cause keloids in people prone to them. Discuss a history of abnormal scarring or an ongoing skin concern with a dermatologist before deciding on further tattooing, and bring that advice into the planning discussion.

Prepare for the consultation

Prepare photos, measurements, and design references

  • One straight-on photo in bright, even light, showing the whole tattoo without filters or glare
  • Side views and a wider photo showing nearby tattoos, surrounding skin, and the body area in a relaxed position
  • The existing tattoo’s approximate height and width, plus the maximum size you would consider for new work
  • Replacement subjects and color references, any details you want to keep, and subjects or colors you do not want
  • Previous cover-ups, reworking, or laser treatments, along with your budget, preferred timing, and questions about the project estimate

Ask whether the proposed subject and colors could work with the existing ink or whether laser lightening should be discussed first. If that option is raised, consult a qualified healthcare professional about the likely result, risks, and healing schedule, and coordinate the timing with your tattoo artist. The FDA’s tattoo removal guidance explains that lasers can lighten or remove ink, that removal requires multiple treatments, and that complete removal is not always possible. Ask for an assessment of your particular tattoo and skin. Include any proposed laser appointments, healing time, and tattoo sessions when discussing your budget and schedule.

Cover-up FAQ

Questions about coverage, size, color, and photos

Can every tattoo be covered?+

Not every replacement concept fits every existing tattoo. Tattooing 101’s design guidance explains that existing work limits the styles and designs available for a cover-up. Ask Josh to assess your tattoo and discuss the proposed subject, palette, and size. Identify your priorities and the changes you would consider.

Does a cover-up have to be much larger?+

A cover-up may extend beyond the original tattoo. Tattooing 101’s design guide includes enlarging the replacement drawing around existing work. Ask to review the proposed outer boundary at the intended size. If you want to retain the same footprint, say so early and discuss which concepts fit. Include nearby tattoos and any planned additions in the discussion.

Can color cover black ink?+

The proposed colors need to be assessed against the darkness of the existing ink. Tattooing 101’s cover-up examples guide cautions that dark old ink can show through lighter-colored work after healing. Discuss the darkest areas of your tattoo, the colors you want, and where lighter details could sit. Ask whether changes to the palette or subject are needed. Request confirmed healed examples of comparable work when available, and ask about any follow-up appointments included in the proposal.

Should I send photos before coming in?+

Yes. Send a straight-on photo of the whole tattoo and wider views showing the body area and nearby work. Include approximate dimensions, the subjects you are considering, and your limits on size or color. Mention previous reworking or laser treatments and any raised or sensitive areas. Use the Email Photos link below to contact the shop, and arrange an in-person assessment as part of planning the project. Ask about the estimate and proposed appointment stages before booking tattoo sessions.
