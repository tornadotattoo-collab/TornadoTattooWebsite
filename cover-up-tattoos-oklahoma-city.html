A better plan for the tattoo you no longer want.

What determines the plan

Cover-ups are design problems, not erasers.

New ink does not make an old tattoo disappear. The replacement design has to redirect attention, place darker values where they can do useful work, and use scale and composition to make the new tattoo read first.

Some existing tattoos provide more flexibility than others. Faded lines, lighter colors, surrounding open skin, and a willingness to increase the overall size can create more options. Dense black areas, heavy scarring, or tight placement may limit which subjects and layouts are practical.

Cover-up factors

What Josh evaluates during consultation.

Clear photos and an in-person look help determine whether a concept can realistically work over the existing piece.

01

Existing Ink

Darkness, line density, color, age, fading, and previous reworking all influence what can be covered.

02

New Scale

The replacement usually needs enough size and surrounding space to create a stronger overall silhouette.

03

Subject Choice

Textures, shadows, overlapping forms, natural shapes, and strong focal points can provide useful design flexibility.

04

Skin Condition

Raised scar tissue, irritation, or damaged skin may affect timing, technique, and whether tattooing should proceed.

Prepare for the consultation

Send information that helps Josh evaluate the project.

  • One straight-on photo in bright, even light
  • Additional angles showing the full placement and nearby open skin
  • Approximate dimensions of the existing tattoo
  • Ideas you are interested in—and subjects you do not want
  • Whether the tattoo has been covered, reworked, or treated before

A consultation may also identify cases where lightening the old tattoo first could create more design options. Tornado Tattoo does not provide removal services, but the condition of the existing piece can still be discussed when planning the next step.

Cover-up FAQ

Realistic answers before you book.

Can every tattoo be covered?+

No. Some tattoos may be too dark, too large for the available space, heavily scarred, or positioned in a way that makes a specific new concept impractical. A consultation is required to evaluate the individual piece.

Does a cover-up have to be much larger?+

Often it needs to extend beyond the original tattoo so the new composition can establish a stronger silhouette and use surrounding skin effectively. The exact increase depends on the old tattoo and the new subject.

Can color cover black ink?+

Color cannot simply overpower dense black ink. Dark values in the new design must be placed strategically, and lighter areas generally need to use open or less-saturated portions of the existing tattoo.

Should I send photos before coming in?+

Yes. Clear photos can help Josh understand the scope, but an in-person consultation may still be needed to inspect texture, scarring, fading, and placement accurately.
