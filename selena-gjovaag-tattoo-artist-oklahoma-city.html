About Selena

From teaching to tattooing.

Tornado Tattoo’s artist biography describes Selena as a former high-school educator who moved into tattooing. It identifies illustrative linework and black-and-gray pieces as strengths. If those approaches interest you, review her work and note the qualities you want to discuss: the shape of an outline, how much shading is used, or the balance between detail and open areas. Bring a specific subject and an approximate size so the conversation can focus on your project.

Try describing your project in one sentence, such as: “I’d like a black-and-gray bird on my outer forearm, about five inches tall, with a clear outline and light shading.” This is an example of the information to include in a request. Then add the details that matter to your idea, such as a particular species or pose. Tell Selena what is essential and what is flexible. If a symbol has personal meaning, describe that meaning in your own words and ask how it could be represented.

Tattoo focus

Illustrative linework, black-and-gray, and your next idea.

The first two areas reflect the styles highlighted in Selena’s biography. Composition and planning give you further questions to discuss when choosing the subject, size, and placement of a tattoo.

01

Illustrative Linework

Linework references can differ in outline thickness, interior detail, and the amount of shading. Getty’s introduction to line and shape explains how lines define edges and vary in width, direction, and length. When reviewing an illustrative design, point out whether you prefer a simple outline, closely spaced detail, or a mix of both. Ask Selena how the subject could be drawn at your intended size and which details would need more room.

02

Black & Gray

For a black-and-gray idea, explain how much shading you want and which parts of your references interest you. Getty’s guide to color and texture describes value as lightness or darkness and explains how line and shading can suggest texture. Use those terms to discuss dark outlines, lighter shaded areas, or repeated marks. Ask to compare relevant examples, including identified healed work if available, and discuss what suits the subject and size you have in mind.

03

Thoughtful Composition

Composition concerns the arrangement of the subjects and surrounding space. Getty’s design principles describe how differences in size, color, shape, and texture can create emphasis. Decide which element you most want someone to notice: a face, a silhouette, lettering, or another part of your idea. Ask how the placement and available area might affect its proportions. Mention nearby tattoos and any plans to extend the design later.

04

Collaborative Planning

Use the consultation to clarify how design review is handled, when changes can be discussed, and what you need to confirm before booking. If your reference includes an existing tattoo, explain whether you want a new piece nearby or a separate design elsewhere. Share timing constraints, such as travel dates. Ask for the shop’s current deposit, rescheduling, and cancellation terms before paying. Confirm the estimate and what it covers so you can compare the proposed plan with your budget.

Consultation process

What to bring when you contact Selena.

Put your description and reference notes together so the request is easy to review. Give an approximate measurement in inches and say which direction the design should face, if you have a preference. Keep a link or source name with each reference and explain the feature you like. If you are considering several placements, mention them. Use the checklist below to prepare your message, then ask which additional information Selena needs to assess the project.

  • The subject, symbol, or story you want represented, including any names or details that must be accurate
  • The body location, approximate measurements, and whether you are considering another placement
  • A few reference images with notes about the linework, shading, or arrangement you like
  • Your preference for black-and-gray, the amount of shading you want, and any other approaches you would like to discuss
  • Clear placement or nearby-tattoo photos if requested, plus your budget and appointment availability

Questions about booking Selena

Before you schedule.

What tattoo styles does Selena focus on?+

Tornado Tattoo’s published biography highlights illustrative linework and black-and-gray pieces. Share the specific subject, size, and placement you are considering, and ask Selena which examples are relevant to it. If you want to discuss another style or color treatment, include a reference and ask about it when contacting the shop. Confirm the direction before scheduling the tattoo.

Can I bring reference images?+

Bring a few reference images and explain what each one contributes to your idea. One might show the subject’s pose, another the kind of shading, and another the arrangement you prefer. Keep the source information with the images. Explain which details are essential and ask how the references could inform a design for your chosen placement and size.

Where can I see Selena’s latest work?+

Use the @seljoart link to view Selena’s Instagram page. When contacting the shop, you can also ask for recent examples relevant to your subject and any identified healed photographs available to review. Keep a link to the specific piece you want to discuss so Selena can see which linework, shading, or composition prompted your interest.

How do I request an appointment with Selena?+

Use the Tornado Tattoo online booking link, call (405) 601-4445, or email tornadotattoookc@gmail.com and request Selena by name. Include the subject, placement, approximate size, and your availability. Ask about the next steps, appointment requirements, and an estimate for the proposed work. The shop’s pricing FAQ says size, color, and completion time affect cost; ask how those factors apply to your idea. Confirm availability and booking details directly with the shop.
