Composition analysis

What the finished tattoo shows.

A responsible cover-up page should not imply a before-and-after transformation when the source photographs do not document one. What the portfolio can demonstrate is Josh’s use of dark structure, layered texture, saturated color, and large compositions—the same kinds of tools that may be useful when planning over existing ink.

The actual plan depends on the old tattoo’s darkness, size, age, color, scar tissue, placement, and surrounding open skin. Clear photographs and an in-person evaluation are required before a cover-up concept can be accepted.

Design challenges

How the composition solves the body area.

01

Dark structure

High-contrast subjects can provide useful areas for placing necessary dark values over existing ink.

02

Scale

A replacement design often needs additional space beyond the old tattoo to establish a stronger silhouette.

03

Texture

Fur, feathers, foliage, ornament, and layered forms can create transitions that a flat subject cannot.

04

Honest limits

Color cannot simply erase dense black ink, and not every placement or concept is a responsible cover-up candidate.
