Contrast in the guitar example

Compare the dark body with the lighter details

The electric guitar has a largely dark body, a long neck with pale fret lines, and lighter strings and rectangular pickup details. Its outer outline and the details within it are separate things to examine when discussing a new subject. Getty’s guide to the elements of art defines value as how light or dark a color is. In a black-and-gray design, compare those values across the subject: the broad dark shapes, midtones, and small highlights. Decide which details matter most to you at the proposed tattoo size.

Cover-up tattooing places a new design over existing work. Tattooing 101’s cover-up design guide describes lining up the proposed drawing with the old tattoo and using its lighter areas when planning highlights. At consultation, ask to review that relationship for your project: where could the darker parts of the new subject sit, which old lines need disguising, and where could lighter details go? Bring photographs that show the entire tattoo and surrounding skin, and ask for an in-person assessment before settling on the replacement concept.

Cover-up design choices

Review contrast, size, texture, and color together

01

Dark and light areas

Mark the darkest lines and filled areas of the tattoo you want covered. Ask how they relate to the proposed subject’s shadows, outlines, and lighter details. The guitar’s broad dark body is a visual reference for this discussion. Explain whether you are comfortable with substantial dark areas in the new design and which features you want to stand out.

02

Size and boundaries

Tattooing 101’s design guidance includes enlarging the new drawing beyond the original tattoo. Measure the existing work and decide how far you are willing to extend the replacement. Ask to review the full outline at the intended size, including its position near joints and neighboring tattoos. Specify whether you want a standalone piece or room for later additions.

03

Texture and pattern

Getty explains that line, color, and shading can depict texture in a two-dimensional image. The owl’s repeated feather shapes and the gemstone’s lattice and scrollwork offer different patterns to compare. Choose a subject whose details you actually want, and ask how those details would align with the existing tattoo. Review the proposed pattern at its full tattoo size.

04

Color and coverage limits

Tattooing 101’s cover-up examples guide cautions that dark existing ink can remain visible through lighter-colored work after healing. Show the colors you prefer and discuss the old tattoo’s darkest areas. Ask which parts of the concept are feasible, whether the palette or subject needs changing, and whether laser lightening should be considered before proceeding. Request healed examples of comparable cover-ups when available.

Prepare for your consultation

Bring the existing tattoo and your replacement ideas

Bring a straight-on photograph of the existing tattoo, side views, and a wider image showing nearby work and available skin. Include approximate measurements and references for the subjects you are considering. Explain which parts of the old tattoo you want disguised, any elements you want to keep, and your limits on size and darkness. Mention previous reworking or laser treatments and any raised, sensitive, or scarred areas. Ask Josh to assess the area in person, discuss your budget and timing, and explain what a proposed estimate includes. Tornado Tattoo’s published pricing FAQ says cost depends on size, color, and completion time. Request a quote and session plan for the actual project you agree on.

Healed results and preparation

Ask about healed examples and possible laser lightening

When reviewing cover-up work, ask for a before photograph and a confirmed healed photograph of the same tattoo, if available. Discuss the visibility of old lines, the new design’s colors, and any follow-up work included in the proposal. If laser lightening is suggested, speak with a qualified healthcare professional about the likely result, risks, and healing schedule, and coordinate the timing with your tattoo artist. The FDA’s tattoo removal guidance explains that lasers can lighten or remove ink, that multiple treatments are needed, and that complete removal is sometimes not possible. Ask for an individual assessment of your existing tattoo and skin before committing to either treatment or tattooing.
