Consultation Guide • Oklahoma City

A useful consultation turns an idea into a tattoo plan.

Bring a description of the tattoo you want, a few reference images, and an approximate placement and size. Write down your questions about price, timing, and preparation. Use this guide to organize what to discuss with Josh before booking the tattoo itself.

Start here

Explain what you want the tattoo to show.

Tornado Tattoo’s custom tattoo information invites clients to bring an idea and discuss examples and pricing. Describe the subject and the features that matter to you: an expression, a flower shape, a color palette, lettering, or a scene. Mark any details you want kept and anything you are happy to change.

Organize your notes around the subject, body area, available space, and style. Add your budget range, schedule limits, and any tattoo already in the proposed area. If you are choosing between two ideas or placements, bring both and explain what you like about each. Ask which decisions need to be settled before a tattoo appointment.

Step by step

Prepare these details before your consultation.

01

1. Define the subject

Start with a sentence naming the main subject and, if relevant, what it means to you. List the details you want included. For a portrait, bring the photo you want discussed. For lettering, write the exact wording, spelling, and punctuation. Identify the essential elements so they are easy to find among your references.

02

2. Choose a placement

Identify the body area and side, such as the outside of the left upper arm. Note whether you want the tattoo visible in everyday clothing, easy to cover for work, or connected to another piece. Wear clothing that gives comfortable access to the area for discussion. Bring up future sleeves or larger projects before deciding where the first tattoo should sit.

03

3. Estimate the size

Measure the height and width you are considering, and give a range if you are flexible. A placement photo with the proposed area marked can make your starting point clear. Ask how the subject and detail would need to change at that size. State any firm size limit before discussing additional elements.

04

4. Gather references

Choose a few images that each explain something specific: the subject, a color combination, the amount of shading, or the lettering. Label what you want to discuss in each image. Keep original photos when possible, and include an example of something you want to avoid. Use other tattoos to describe your preferences without requesting a duplicate of another artist’s work.

05

5. Disclose existing work

For a cover-up or rework, prepare clear, unfiltered photos of the existing tattoo and one wider view of the surrounding area. Note whether you want parts retained, covered, or added to, and mention any scars in the placement. Discuss medical concerns with a healthcare professional; the FDA’s tattoo safety guidance describes skin reactions and when to seek medical advice.

06

6. Discuss scheduling and price

The shop’s published pricing information lists a $50 minimum and says cost depends on size, color, and time. Bring your budget range and ask what the estimate covers, whether more than one session is expected, and how payment works. Confirm any deposit, cancellation, or rescheduling terms before committing. Ask how much time to reserve for the consultation and the tattoo appointment.

Useful references

Choose references you can explain.

  • Choose clear images where the subject is easy to see. Use the original photo if you have it.
  • Explain what each reference is for: the subject, pose, mood, arrangement, or colors. Point out the part you want discussed.
  • Include examples of the line weight, shading, color, or black-and-gray treatment you prefer.
  • Keep the strongest references together and label them. Add a note if you are weighing different directions.
  • List features you want to avoid, including unwanted text, backgrounds, colors, or expressions.

Before booking

Confirm the details before booking.

  • Discuss a design using your references. Do not assume another artist’s tattoo will be reproduced line for line.
  • Show any tattoo already in the planned area, including one you want partly covered. Include a wider placement photo.
  • Keep photos free of filters that alter color or skin texture. Ask the shop how it wants images submitted.
  • State your budget range and confirm what the estimate includes. Ask how design changes would affect that estimate.
  • Do not arrive intoxicated for a tattoo appointment. Review Mayo Clinic’s tattoo safety guidance and ask the shop about preparation instructions.

Common questions

Questions about your consultation.

Do I need to know the exact size?+

Bring your best estimate of the height and width, along with a placement photo if useful. Say how much flexibility you have. Ask the artist what details fit within that space and what would need simplifying if you choose a smaller size.

Can I change my idea after the consultation?+

Contact the shop as soon as you know what has changed. Describe the new subject, placement, size, or style, and ask whether it affects the design work, quote, or appointment. Before booking, clarify how revisions are handled. Keep the updated direction in writing so there is one version to discuss.

Should I email photos before visiting?+

Ask the shop whether it wants photos before the consultation and which contact method to use. For cover-ups and reworks, prepare a close view and a wider placement photo without filters. Ask whether an in-person assessment is also needed. Bring medical questions about your skin to a healthcare professional.

Does a consultation guarantee the project will be accepted?+

Confirm with the artist whether they can take on your proposed design and placement before reserving tattoo time. Ask what needs to be agreed next and which details remain undecided. Keep the project discussion, price estimate, and booking confirmation together so you can refer back to them.
