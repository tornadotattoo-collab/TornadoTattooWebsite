Tattoo Aftercare Guide

Protect the work while your skin does the healing.

Follow the written instructions Josh or your tattoo artist gives you at the appointment. This page provides general education and does not replace medical advice or artist-specific bandage instructions.

Book a ConsultationCall the Shop

Start here

Your artist’s instructions come first.

Bandage type, tattoo size, placement, skin sensitivity, and the way the session was completed can change the first steps. Do not remove or replace a medical-style adhesive bandage on a schedule copied from another person; follow the instructions given for your tattoo.

General healing care focuses on clean hands, gentle washing, light moisture, breathable clothing, and avoiding soaking, friction, picking, and ultraviolet exposure while the skin is recovering.

Step by step

Use this checklist before and after the appointment.

01

Clean hands first

Wash your hands before touching the tattoo or bandage. Avoid unnecessary contact from other people, pets, gym surfaces, and shared towels.

02

Wash gently

When instructed to wash the tattoo, use lukewarm water, a gentle touch, and a mild fragrance-free cleanser. Pat dry with a clean disposable paper towel or allow it to air dry.

03

Use a light layer

Apply only the product and amount recommended by the artist. Heavy layers can trap moisture and stick to clothing.

04

Avoid soaking

Stay out of pools, hot tubs, lakes, rivers, and baths while the tattoo is healing. Showering is different from prolonged submersion.

05

Reduce friction

Choose clean, loose clothing that does not rub or stick to the tattoo. Avoid activities that repeatedly stretch or abrade the area.

06

Leave flakes and scabs alone

Itching and flaking can occur during normal healing. Do not scratch, peel, or pick at the tattoo.

07

Protect healed skin

After healing, broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher and sensible clothing help reduce ultraviolet fading.

Usually expected

Normal healing versus a reason to get medical help

  • Mild tenderness, redness, or swelling that gradually improves
  • A small amount of clear fluid during early healing
  • Itching, flaking, or light scabbing as the surface recovers
  • Temporary dullness while new surface skin forms

Get medical help

Do not ask a webpage to diagnose an infection.

  • Redness that spreads or becomes darker instead of improving
  • Pain or swelling that worsens rather than subsides
  • Pus, open sores, foul odor, fever, chills, or red streaking
  • Trouble breathing, intense swelling, hives, dizziness, or chest tightness

Common questions

Clear answers without pretending every person heals or plans the same way.

How long does a tattoo take to heal?+

Surface healing often takes roughly two weeks, but deeper recovery can take longer. Size, placement, skin, health, and aftercare all affect timing.

Can I work out after getting tattooed?+

Ask the artist about the exact placement and activity. Friction, stretching, sweat, shared surfaces, and tight clothing can interfere with early healing.

When can I swim?+

Avoid pools, hot tubs, lakes, rivers, and other soaking until the tattoo is healed and your artist says it is safe.

What should I do if I think it is infected?+

Contact a healthcare professional promptly. Seek urgent care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, fever, spreading redness, significant swelling, or signs of a serious allergic reaction.
