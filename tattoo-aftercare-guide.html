Tattoo Aftercare Guide

Protect the work while your skin does the healing.

Use the written aftercare instructions you receive at the appointment for the bandage and products used on your tattoo. This guide covers everyday care, changes you may notice while healing, and symptoms that need medical attention. Follow a healthcare professional’s advice if you have a medical concern.

Start here

Start with the instructions for your bandage.

Before leaving the shop, confirm what kind of dressing you have, when it should come off, how to remove it, and whether it needs replacing. Keep those directions with you, and ask for clarification if a step is unclear. Someone else’s aftercare routine may involve a different dressing or product; use the directions for the bandage on your skin.

After removing the bandage as directed, care generally involves gentle washing, a mild moisturizer, clothing that avoids rubbing, and keeping the area out of sun and soaking water. Ask your artist about routine care. If the tattoo seems infected, develops a rash, or is not healing as expected, contact a healthcare professional.

Step by step

Make gentle care part of your daily routine.

01

Clean hands first

Wash your hands before and after handling the bandage or caring for the tattoo. Follow the CDC’s handwashing steps: use soap and clean running water, scrub for at least 20 seconds, then rinse and dry. Include between your fingers and under your nails. Wash again after handling pets or touching dirty surfaces.

02

Wash gently

Once the bandage is off as instructed, wash with warm water and a mild cleanser without fragrance. Use a light touch rather than scrubbing. In the shower, avoid directing the water stream at the tattoo. Pat the area dry with a clean paper towel instead of rubbing it.

03

Use a mild moisturizer

Use a mild moisturizer or the aftercare product identified in your written instructions, following the amount and frequency given. Ask for the exact product name if you are unsure. If new redness, bumps, or a rash develops after applying a product, contact a healthcare professional about the reaction.

04

Avoid soaking

While the tattoo is healing, stay out of pools, hot tubs, lakes, rivers, and baths. Shower gently instead of soaking the area. Plan swimming around healing rather than a fixed date; Mayo Clinic’s aftercare guidance advises avoiding swimming during recovery.

05

Reduce friction

Choose clean, loose clothing that lets the area breathe and does not stick to the tattoo. Check where waistbands, bra straps, socks, sleeves, or other edges sit against the tattooed area. Ask your artist how to manage clothing or work equipment that would rub the placement while it heals.

06

Leave flakes and scabs alone

Itching, peeling, and some scabbing can happen during healing. Leave loose skin and scabs to come away on their own. Avoid scratching, scrubbing, or exfoliating the area. Ask a clinician about itching that comes with a new rash or worsening symptoms.

07

Protect healed skin

Keep a healing tattoo out of direct sunlight; use shade and clothing that does not rub the area. Once the skin is healed, use a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen rated SPF 30 or higher when exposed outdoors. Apply it 15 minutes before going outside and reapply at least every two hours, following the product’s directions after swimming or sweating. Avoid tanning beds and sunlamps, which can fade tattoo ink.

Usually expected

Changes you may notice during healing.

  • Mild soreness or localized redness and swelling can occur early on. These symptoms should ease rather than grow worse.
  • A little clear seepage can occur in a new tattoo. Drainage with pus or an unusual odor needs medical attention.
  • Some itching, peeling, or scab formation can be part of healing. Leave the surface alone instead of picking it.
  • These examples cannot confirm that a tattoo is healing normally. If you are unsure about a change, contact a healthcare professional.

Get medical help

Know when to call a doctor or 911.

  • Contact a doctor promptly if redness is spreading outward or deepening in color instead of settling.
  • Get medical advice for pain that intensifies, increasing swelling, skin that feels hot, or red streaks extending away from the tattoo.
  • See a doctor right away for pus, an open sore, fever, chills, or foul-smelling drainage. Do not wait for a shop appointment.
  • Call 911 for trouble breathing, swelling of the tongue or throat, fainting, or a suspected serious allergic reaction. Hives with chest tightness or dizziness can also be an emergency.

Common questions

Aftercare and healing questions.

How long does a tattoo take to heal?+

Estimates vary. Mayo Clinic describes about two weeks for many tattoos; Cleveland Clinic notes that larger tattoos and deeper layers of skin can take months. Treat these as broad guidance rather than a deadline for your tattoo. Follow the care directions you receive, and contact a clinician if healing seems stalled or symptoms worsen.

Can I work out after getting tattooed?+

Ask your artist when to resume your particular workout, and describe any movements, clothing, or equipment that would rub or press on the tattoo. Bring this up before the appointment if training is part of your daily routine. This guide does not set a universal return date. A healthcare professional should address any medical concerns about exercise during recovery.

When can I swim?+

Wait until the tattoo has healed before swimming or soaking, including in hot tubs and natural water. Mayo Clinic advises avoiding these during healing. Ask your artist if you are unsure about routine aftercare; if healing is abnormal or you have concerning symptoms, get medical advice.

What should I do if I think it is infected?+

Contact a doctor or dermatologist promptly. Give them the tattoo date and a description of what changed. Let the artist know; the FDA advises asking for the ink’s brand, color, and lot or batch number if available. Call 911 if you have breathing trouble or other signs of a serious allergic reaction.
