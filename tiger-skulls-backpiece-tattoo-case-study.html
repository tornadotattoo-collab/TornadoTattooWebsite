Composition analysis

Read the tattoo from top to bottom.

Getty’s guide to the elements of art defines value as how light or dark a color is. In this photograph, the tiger’s stripes sit against lighter areas of the muzzle and forehead. The skull below has dark eye sockets, a shaded nose cavity, and lighter surfaces across the brow and teeth. Compare those larger areas before looking closely at the fur and whiskers.

The tiger’s open mouth sits directly above the skull’s brow. A geometric band crosses the upper back above the ears, while curling forms repeat along the outer edges. Getty’s guide to design principles explains emphasis through differences in size, shape, texture, and other elements. To study this image, compare the size of the two faces with the smaller repeated shapes around them.

Details to look for

Compare the large shapes and smaller marks.

01

Light and dark areas

Compare the tiger’s dark stripes with its lighter brow and muzzle, then the skull’s sockets with its lighter forehead. The image contains broad shaded areas as well as narrow lines. For your own design, ask where the deepest shading and open skin would go and which features you want emphasized.

02

The skull below the tiger

The large skull occupies the lower center, with its forehead directly beneath the tiger’s open mouth. Its eye sockets, nose cavity, and row of teeth are visible as separate features. If you want to combine subjects, bring references for each and discuss their relative size and position. Explain any personal meaning in your own words.

03

Depth

The skull’s eye sockets contain darker shading than its brow and cheek surfaces. Narrow dark bands also run along the curled side forms. Getty’s art guide describes shading as a way to depict three-dimensional space. In a consultation, point to the shaded cavities or curved shapes you like and ask how similar visual effects could work with your chosen subject.

04

Scale

The tiger’s head spans much of the upper back, while the skull extends down the center toward the lower back. Fine whisker lines cross both cheek areas, and teeth appear in both faces. Look at the whole arrangement and then the smaller details. When discussing size, name the features you want included and ask how they fit the available space.

Planning a related project

Choose the features you want to discuss.

Bring references for your chosen subject and mark what interests you in this image: the black-and-gray shading, the stacked animal and skull, the curling border, or the amount of space it covers. Name the features you want included and what you would leave out. Share your planned body area, approximate dimensions, any existing tattoos nearby, and whether the work is meant to connect to a sleeve or another piece.
