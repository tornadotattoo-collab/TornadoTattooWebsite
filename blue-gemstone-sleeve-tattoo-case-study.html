Gemstone shading

How light and dark suggest a faceted surface

Facets are flat surfaces cut at different angles on a gemstone. The Gemological Institute of America explains these features in its guide to gem cutting styles. In visual art, value describes how light or dark a color is. This tattoo contains darker blue areas, lighter blue planes, and pale angular shapes within the central stone, with fine light lines marking parts of the facet pattern. These visible differences give us specific details to examine when discussing the design.

[Inference] The pale facets and dark outer frame make the blue gemstone read as a raised, reflective centerpiece. The straight lattice lines and broad curved scrolls provide a contrast in shape around the central oval. Together, the oval frame and small circular edge details suggest a jewelry setting. Getty’s guide to the elements of art explains how shading can suggest depth and how artists depict surface texture.

Details to study

Four considerations for an ornamental gemstone sleeve

01

A blue focal point

The central gemstone is the largest blue shape visible in the photograph. Its interior includes both dark and light regions. For a new design, discuss how much of the arm the stone should occupy, which blue shades you prefer, and how much ornament should surround it. If you want a specific gemstone represented, identify it during the consultation; this project’s stone type is not specified.

02

Separation between stone and frame

[Inference] The dark oval outline gives the pale edges of the gemstone a contrasting background, while the small light marks around the rim suggest a decorative border. For your own tattoo, ask to review the stone, frame, and surrounding pattern at the proposed size. Compare the detail close up and from farther away to decide whether the shapes are as distinct as you want them to be.

03

Lattice, scrollwork, and open space

The repeated diagonals form a lattice around the gemstone. Curved scrolls, round accents, and floral outlines add different shapes. Getty’s design vocabulary describes repetition as a way to create unity and variety as the use of different elements. For a related arm tattoo, discuss the balance you prefer between repeated pattern, larger ornament, and areas of visible skin.

04

Placement around the arm

A close-up shows one view of a curved body area. Decide where you want the main gemstone and how the surrounding pattern should continue. Ask to review the proposed design from the front, outer arm, inner arm, and back, with the elbow bent and extended. Discuss its boundaries at the wrist or shoulder and whether existing tattoos need to be incorporated.

Planning a related project

What to bring to a gemstone tattoo consultation

Bring references and explain what attracts you: the blue color, faceted stone, oval frame, lattice, scrollwork, or floral details. Decide whether you are considering a forearm piece, half sleeve, or full sleeve, and bring clear photos of any existing arm tattoos. Discuss size, placement, detail, and room for future additions. If you want a particular gemstone or personal meaning included, say so explicitly. Ask Josh about the proposed stages, session lengths, healing intervals, and estimate for your design. Tornado Tattoo’s published FAQ says pricing depends on size, color, and the time needed to complete the tattoo.

Tattoo care

Caring for a blue gemstone tattoo

Follow the aftercare instructions provided for your tattoo. While it heals, Cleveland Clinic advises avoiding direct sunlight, picking or scratching, tight clothing, and soaking, and waiting until the tattoo is fully healed before applying sunscreen. For healed tattooed skin, the American Academy of Dermatology recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, applied before going outdoors and reapplied at least every two hours. UV exposure can fade some tattoo inks. Ask to see clearly identified healed examples when discussing the appearance and maintenance of a future project.
