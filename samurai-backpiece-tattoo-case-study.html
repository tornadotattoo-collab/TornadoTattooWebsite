Composition analysis

Armor, architecture, and contrast.

The warrior’s helmet rises into the upper-right area of the picture. Below it, the face has visible teeth and curling decorative forms around the chin. Rows of rectangular sections cover the shoulder, and a grid of plates appears across the torso. On the left, the building’s wide roof edges extend over windows and stacked levels. The mountain shapes appear behind these subjects. For historical armor references, the Arizona State Museum’s helmet and half-mask example identifies a kabuto (helmet) and a mempo (half-mask). Museum photographs are useful when discussing the kind of headgear, mask, or armor you want. Use a reference’s caption to name what interests you, and keep its source with the image.

The image contains both broad dark areas and small repeated marks. Around the helmet, dark curved bands sit beside pale projecting shapes; the shoulder plates contain short, closely spaced details. Getty’s guide to the elements of art explains value as lightness or darkness and describes how line, color, and shading suggest texture. Use those terms to explain what you like about this piece. You might want the contrast around the face, the patterned armor, or the building’s roof shapes in your references. Specify which details are essential and which can change with the size and placement of your design.

Composition and planning

Questions to bring to a backpiece consultation.

01

Focal hierarchy

Size, color, shape, and texture can create emphasis, as Getty’s guide to design principles describes. In this photograph, the armored figure occupies a larger area than the building, and the helmet includes strong light-dark contrasts. Decide whether you want the face, the armor, or the setting to receive the most attention in your own project. Ask how that choice would affect the relative sizes of the main subjects and the amount of background detail.

02

Architecture and landscape

The building’s long roof edges and stacked levels differ from the small repeated shapes in the armor. Mountain forms extend behind both. If a particular castle, temple, or landscape matters to you, bring a reference that identifies it and point out the features you want represented. Ask how much of the setting can fit around your main figure. A roof silhouette, facade, and complete building are different requests, with different amounts of detail to accommodate.

03

Placement across the back

Decide how much of the upper back, lower back, and sides you want covered before discussing the subjects. Identify the desired top and bottom boundaries and whether the tattoo should extend toward the shoulders or existing work. Bring current placement photographs if requested, and ask to review how the layout sits on your back before tattooing begins. Make clear whether you are planning one backpiece or a composition that may extend later, so the intended extent can be considered from the start.

04

Session planning

Request a session and cost estimate for the design you are discussing. Ask what the estimate includes, how appointments are expected to be divided, and whether changes to size or detail would affect it. Tornado Tattoo’s pricing information says size, color, and completion time affect price and that large or complicated designs may need multiple sessions. That provides planning context; this photograph includes no cost or appointment record for the pictured tattoo.

Planning a related project

Choose the details that matter to your idea.

Bring a short description of the subject and two or three references that explain your direction. For a warrior design, useful notes include the pose, helmet shape, facial expression, armor details, and the amount of architecture or landscape you want around the figure. If you are referencing a named person or a particular building, include the name and source image so those details can be discussed. Tell Josh which elements of this example interest you and how you would like your project to differ. Include the placement, approximate size, existing tattoos nearby, preferred color treatment, and budget. Ask what needs to be simplified or enlarged to suit the available space.
