Project Study • Traditional Color

Traditional tiger: a bold silhouette shaped for the shoulder.

Josh Poindexter’s traditional-inspired color tiger has an orange face, broad black stripes, pale brows and whiskers, and an open mouth with prominent fangs. Pink flowers and curling black and gray shapes surround the head. This study examines the details visible in one front view and the questions to consider when planning a related design.

Composition analysis

What the photograph shows.

This project is described here as traditional-inspired color. The New York Historical’s account of American traditional tattooing identifies strong black outlines and a limited palette as features of that style. In this photograph, the tiger’s orange face is crossed by broad black stripes, with pale marks along several stripe edges. Purple details appear in the nose and open mouth, and pink flowers with green leaves sit around the head. Those are useful specifics to identify when choosing a reference for a color tattoo.

Getty’s guide to the elements of art distinguishes color hue from value, meaning how light or dark a color is. Here, the pale brows, whiskers, and teeth sit beside the black stripes and dark mouth edge. The photograph also shows gray shading in the curling border, especially along the broad forms at the top and sides. When discussing a related drawing, note both the colors you prefer and the arrangement of lighter and darker areas.

Design details

Look closely at the head, border, and placement.

01

Silhouette

The outside shape includes a rounded ear at the top right of the photograph, pointed cheek fur, and curls around the lower jaw. The striped face sits inside this larger border. If you use the piece as a reference, decide whether you want a head alone or surrounding shapes as well, and bring that preference to the consultation.

02

Placement

The broad head extends across the upper chest, and its upper-right ear sits toward the shoulder. Part of another tattoo is visible along the left edge of the photograph. For a related piece, bring approximate width and height measurements and show nearby work. Ask about the space around the border and any area you want to reserve for future tattoos.

03

Color and lightness

The orange forehead and cheeks are divided by thick black stripes. The pale brows and whiskers cross several dark and orange areas, while purple appears around the nose and inside the mouth. Small green leaves and the yellow flower center add further color below the jaw. These visible areas give you a concrete palette to discuss, including which colors you want repeated, reduced, or omitted.

04

Detail and size

The photograph is a close view of the muzzle, whiskers, and teeth. It provides no measurement for the tattoo itself. If you are planning a smaller design, identify the features you most want to keep and ask how much room they need. Review the proposed drawing at the intended dimensions and discuss which details would be simplified for that size.

Planning a related project

Turn the reference into a clear brief.

Bring a short brief with your subject, preferred expression, body area, and approximate measurements. Point to the features you like in this tiger: the broad stripes, pale brows, open mouth, flower accents, or curling border. Separate essential details from optional additions, and include any lettering exactly as you want it spelled. Mention existing tattoos, plans to add more work nearby, and your color preferences. Ask whether the proposed elements fit the chosen area and what changes are worth considering before an appointment is scheduled.

Build your own project

Plan your own traditional-inspired tiger.

Start with the body area, approximate size, and design features you want to discuss with Josh. Include a budget or target date if either affects your plans. At the consultation, ask for the proposed size and color direction, a price estimate, and the expected appointment requirements for your drawing. Confirm those details with the shop before booking tattooing time.
