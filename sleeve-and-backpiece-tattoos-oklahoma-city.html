Sleeves and Backpieces • Oklahoma City

Large-scale tattoos need one plan before they become many sessions.

Start a sleeve or backpiece by defining the area you want covered and the subjects you want included. This page brings together military-inspired and floral sleeve photographs with a tiger-and-skull backpiece example by Josh Poindexter. Compare the coverage, color, shading, and amount of detail that interest you. Then request a consultation with Josh at Tornado Tattoo in Oklahoma City to discuss your own design, an estimate, and an appointment plan.

Define the project scope

Choose the coverage and main subjects.

For a sleeve, describe the top and bottom boundaries you have in mind and whether you want the design to continue around the arm. For a backpiece, discuss the upper and lower edges, the area toward each shoulder, and any extension toward the sides. Include your preference for a single scene, several separate subjects, or a design that could be extended later. Ask to review the proposed coverage on your body. Use an overall placement view as well as close details when comparing examples, so you can discuss the proportions of the whole project.

If you already have tattoos in the area, identify which pieces you want to keep and which you would like to discuss changing. Ask Josh to assess the available space around them and whether your idea could include shared background, separate borders, or another arrangement. If you are considering rework or a cover-up, include clear photographs of the existing work, say what outcome you want, and ask for an assessment. If you expect to add more tattoos later, mention that plan before agreeing on the boundaries of the current piece.

Design priorities

Questions to settle before drawing.

Use these four areas to discuss the proposed design and what committing to a larger project would involve.

01

Master Composition

Choose the subject or feature you want to receive the most attention. Getty’s guide to design principles explains how differences in size, color, shape, and texture can create emphasis. Ask how your main subject would relate in size to the supporting elements and background. For a scene with several figures, decide which figure matters most to you. For a floral design, identify the flowers or insects you want emphasized and any details you are open to simplifying.

02

Anatomy and Flow

Discuss which parts of the tattoo you want visible from the front, side, and back of the chosen area. For a sleeve, ask where key details would sit around the shoulder, elbow, and wrist. For a backpiece, review the position of the main subject and the boundaries toward the shoulders and waist. Ask to see the proposed placement in more than one pose. If lettering, a face, or a particular emblem is essential, point it out during that review.

03

Session Staging

Ask how the proposed work would be divided into appointments and what each estimate covers. Discuss the expected length of a sitting, your availability, and travel or work commitments. Request the shop’s guidance on aftercare and when to return for further work. Confirm how design review, deposits, and rescheduling are handled before booking. If you have a target date, raise it during the consultation and ask whether it is realistic for the design being discussed.

04

Continuity

Compare the line thickness, dark and light areas, colors, and background in your references. Getty’s guide to the elements of art describes value as lightness or darkness and explains how line, color, and shading suggest texture. Use those terms to discuss differences between the images you bring. Ask which features should repeat across the proposed design and where you want variation, especially if it includes existing tattoos or several different subjects.

References, cost, and availability

Prepare the details that affect your project.

Bring a few references and explain what each contributes to your idea. One might show the subject’s pose, another the color treatment, and another the amount of background you prefer. Keep the source information with the images. If your design includes a particular emblem, flower species, name, or date, provide an exact reference and check those details during design review. Rank your essential elements so you can discuss what to enlarge, simplify, or leave out if the available area cannot accommodate every request.

Tornado Tattoo’s pricing FAQ identifies size, color, and completion time as cost factors. Request an estimate for the scope you are discussing and ask what it includes, how payment is handled, and whether the estimate covers the entire project or an individual sitting. Share your budget and the dates you can attend. If you are comparing a larger version with a smaller one, ask how changing the dimensions or amount of detail would affect both cost and scheduling.

Prepare for consultation

Bring a clear brief and useful placement references.

  • Clear placement photographs from several angles if requested, including the proposed top and bottom boundaries
  • Your main subjects, ranked by importance, with accurate references for essential names, dates, or symbols
  • Existing tattoos you want to keep, include, change, or discuss working around
  • Your preference for color or black-and-gray, plus examples of the linework and shading you like
  • Your budget, appointment availability, travel commitments, and any target date to discuss

Questions clients ask

How many sessions does a sleeve or backpiece take?+

Request an estimate for your particular design. The shop’s appointment FAQ notes that large, colorful, or complicated tattoos may need multiple sessions. Ask how the proposed work would be divided, how long each sitting is expected to last, and what could change the estimate. Confirm the shop’s guidance on returning for further work. A gallery photograph does not establish the session count for the tattoo pictured.

Can a sleeve be built one tattoo at a time?+

Discuss whether you want separate pieces or a larger composition developed across appointments. If you already have individual tattoos, show the entire area and identify which ones you want to keep. Ask about the remaining space, possible background, and where additional subjects could sit. If your eventual goal is a sleeve, mention that before adding the next piece so the larger scope can be considered during the discussion.

Do I need every detail decided before consultation?+

You can start the conversation with the subjects you want, a placement, and a few references. State which details you have decided and which questions are still open. Ask Josh to discuss the possible coverage, relative sizes of the main elements, and amount of background. If several ideas matter equally to you, say so and ask how they might be arranged. Confirm the proposed direction and design-review process before booking tattooing time.

Can large work combine styles?+

Bring examples of each approach you want to discuss and identify the feature you like in each one. You might prefer the outline in one reference and the shading or color in another. Ask Josh which differences could be carried into the proposed design and which would need adjustment. Discuss how the subjects, background, and existing tattoos would relate within the available space. Confirm the combination being proposed for your project before scheduling it.
