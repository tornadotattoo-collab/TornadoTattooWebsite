Black-and-Gray Tattoos

Explore light and dark areas, shaded transitions, and the relationship between the main subject and its background. The guide discusses placement, scale, and detail, with links to backpiece, wolf, and guitar examples from Josh’s portfolio. Use it to compare the amount of texture and shadow you prefer. For your own design, identify the features you want visible at the proposed size and bring photos of any tattoos nearby. Ask how the new work could fit the available area.

Color Tattoos

Compare full-color work and selected color accents through Josh’s portfolio examples. The guide looks at palette, lightness and darkness, placement, and references, including owl, floral, moth, and sugar-skull subjects. Decide which colors you want in your own piece and which details matter most. Bring images that show the palette you like, plus photos of nearby tattoos if the new design needs to connect with them. Use the examples to prepare questions about color, size, and composition.

Sleeves and Backpieces

Read about planning a larger body area, choosing the main subjects, and discussing how separate sections could connect. The guide includes military and floral sleeve images alongside backpiece planning questions. Show the full arm or back in your placement photos, including existing tattoos, and explain your intended boundaries. Rank the subjects you most want included and discuss your availability for appointments. Ask for a project-specific estimate, proposed stages, and guidance on healing between sessions before committing to a schedule.

Illustrative Tattoos

Start with a recognizable subject, such as a lighthouse, animal, object, or plant, and explore how it could become an illustrative tattoo. The guide includes lighthouse, apple-branch, and wolf-headdress examples and discusses subject choice, surrounding shapes, color, and detail. Use references to explain the drawing style you prefer: visible outlines, simplified forms, shaded texture, or photographic detail. Tell Josh which parts of the subject matter to you and what supporting elements you would like to discuss.

Consultation Guide

Prepare a brief with your subject, preferred body placement, approximate dimensions, and a few useful references. The guide walks through those choices, existing tattoos in the area, scheduling, and price questions. Explain what you like about each image and identify any details you want included or avoided. Bring photos showing the proposed placement and nearby work. Use the consultation to discuss the design direction, estimate, appointment plan, and any changes needed before booking the tattoo.

Tattoo Aftercare Guide

Read general information about bandage instructions, gentle cleaning, moisturizing, clothing, and activities to discuss while a tattoo heals. The guide also covers sun protection for healed skin and signs that need medical attention, with references from dermatology and healthcare organizations. Ask your artist for written instructions for your tattoo and bandage, and clarify anything you do not understand before leaving the appointment. Use the guide as a reference, and contact a healthcare professional about concerning symptoms.
