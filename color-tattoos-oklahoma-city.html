Choosing your colors

Start with the subject and the colors you want.

Your references can include tattoos, botanical photographs, illustrations, or objects with colors you like. Explain what each reference contributes: the red of a flower, the blue of a gemstone, or the dark outlines around an ornamental shape. Josh’s work shown here includes pieces with several color families and a gemstone design centered on blue. Decide whether you want color throughout the tattoo or concentrated in a few specific areas, and list any colors you want left out.

Color has several qualities to discuss. Getty’s guide to the elements of art distinguishes hue, the color itself; value, how light or dark it is; and intensity, how bright or dull it appears. In the gemstone photograph, for example, pale blue and deeper blue appear within the same faceted shape. When describing your preferences, point out both the colors and their lightness. Ask to review the proposed palette with the subject, size, and your skin tone in mind.

Design priorities

Four choices to discuss at your consultation

Use these questions to explain the look you want and review the proposed design at its intended size.

01

Palette

Choose the colors you want to feature and identify any you dislike. For a floral tattoo, bring references for the petals and foliage separately. For an ornamental piece, point out the colors you want repeated in its borders or patterns. Ask how those choices could work within your design.

02

Contrast

Compare the light and dark areas as well as the hues. The owl’s pale face, dark eyes, and colored feathers offer one example in this portfolio. When reviewing your own design, check the outlines, shading, and spaces between details. Ask which features remain distinct at the proposed size.

03

Placement

Specify the body area and approximate dimensions. For an arm piece, discuss the front, side, and back views and where the main subject should sit. Mention nearby tattoos, the boundaries you want, and any plans to extend the piece later.

04

Healing and sun exposure

Discuss how you can care for the chosen area during healing, including clothing and activities that could involve rubbing or soaking. Ask for aftercare instructions for your appointment. For healed tattooed skin, the American Academy of Dermatology recommends sun protection because UV light can fade some tattoo inks.

Size, placement, and scope

Plan the whole area, including any future additions.

Bring approximate height and width measurements and a clear photograph of the area. Explain whether you want a standalone tattoo, half sleeve, full sleeve, or chest piece. For an arm design, ask to review the proposed placement with the elbow bent and extended. If the subject includes a face, flowers, wings, or lettering, identify the details you want included and ask whether the available space supports them. Show any existing tattoos that need to be considered alongside the new work.

If you are considering a larger project, mention future additions before choosing the first section’s boundaries. Bring your budget and preferred timing to the same discussion. Tornado Tattoo’s published pricing FAQ says cost depends on size, color, and completion time. Request an estimate for your specific idea and ask what the proposed appointments would cover, how healing fits between them, and what is included in the quoted price.

Prepare for consultation

Prepare your color tattoo references and questions

  • Reference images of the subjects and colors you like, with a note explaining what you want from each example
  • Preferred colors and colors to leave out; mention whether you want a broad palette or a few color accents
  • The body location, approximate height and width, and clear photos showing the area in a relaxed position
  • Photos of nearby tattoos and any plans to continue into a sleeve, chest piece, or larger connected project
  • Your budget, preferred timing, and questions about the estimate, appointment stages, healing, and ongoing care

Questions clients ask

How do I choose a palette for my tattoo?+

Bring the subject and palette you have in mind, then ask how they could fit the proposed size. Compare examples with dark outlines, extensive color, or a few colored accents, and explain which approach you prefer. If a small design contains many separate details, ask to see it at the intended dimensions before deciding. Use that discussion to review which colors and features should take priority.

What should I ask about color on my skin tone?+

Ask to see healed examples on skin tones similar to yours, if available, and confirm when those photographs were taken. Cleveland Clinic explains that tattoo pigment is injected into the dermis, a layer beneath the skin’s surface. Use healed photographs to discuss the appearance you want, and ask which color choices Josh recommends for your subject, size, and placement.

How should I care for a new and healed color tattoo?+

Follow the aftercare instructions provided for your tattoo. Cleveland Clinic advises avoiding picking, scratching, soaking, direct sunlight, and sunscreen on a tattoo that is still healing. Once fully healed, the American Academy of Dermatology recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen rated SPF 30 or higher, applied 15 minutes before going outdoors and reapplied at least every two hours. UV light can fade some tattoo inks; make sun protection part of your long-term care. Contact a healthcare professional for worsening pain, spreading redness, fever, or other concerning symptoms.

How can I plan color work around an existing tattoo?+

Bring clear photographs showing the existing tattoo and the surrounding area, and ask Josh to assess the options in person. Explain whether you want to extend the subject, repeat particular colors, add a separate piece nearby, or discuss a cover-up. Include your preferred boundaries and any later additions. Ask what can be incorporated into the proposed design and whether the estimate involves changes to the existing work as well as the new tattoo.
