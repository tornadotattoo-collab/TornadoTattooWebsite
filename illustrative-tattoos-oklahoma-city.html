Josh’s planning approach

Start with the subject, then build the composition around it.

Illustrative work can start with almost anything: a landmark, animal, object, botanical element, character, place, or personal symbol. The first job is deciding what the viewer should read first and how much supporting detail the placement can handle.

Josh uses the body area, scale, contrast, and color relationships to keep the main idea clear instead of letting every reference compete for attention.

Design priorities

What Josh evaluates before drawing.

These choices help the artwork stay readable as a tattoo instead of turning into a crowded illustration.

01

Primary Subject

The design needs one clear focal point before secondary elements are added.

02

Shape and Flow

The composition should follow the body area instead of feeling like a flat image pasted onto the skin.

03

Color and Contrast

Color choices, dark values, and open skin work together to separate major forms and keep the image readable.

04

Useful Detail

Small details only stay when they help the subject. Scale determines how much information the tattoo can realistically support.

Placement and execution

Illustrative work can be bold, colorful, symbolic, or highly specific.

A lighthouse, flower, animal, object, or place can work as the main subject without needing to fit a traditional category. What matters is whether the design has a strong silhouette, enough scale, and a composition that works on the selected body area.

Bring the references that explain the idea. Josh can use them to identify the parts worth keeping and simplify anything that would make the tattoo harder to read.

Prepare for consultation

Bring the idea and the references that explain it.

  • The main subject you want the tattoo built around
  • Reference images that show the shape, mood, or details you like
  • The body location and approximate size
  • Whether you want full color, limited color, or black-and-gray
  • Any existing tattoos the new piece needs to work around

Questions clients ask

Does illustrative mean one specific tattoo style?+

No. It describes custom work built around a clear visual subject. The execution can lean bold, colorful, traditional, painterly, graphic, or somewhere between.

Can I combine several references?+

Yes. References can explain different parts of the idea, but the final tattoo still needs one composition that works on the body.

Can the design be mostly color?+

Yes. Josh can plan the palette around separation, contrast, placement, and the amount of detail the tattoo needs.

What if my idea does not fit a named style?+

That is fine. Start with the subject and placement. The consultation can determine the best visual direction for the final tattoo.
